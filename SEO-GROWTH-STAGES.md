# SEO and lead-generation growth stages

## Scope decision

The website will focus on exactly three service offers:

1. **Landingsside** — one focused page for one offer, service, or campaign.
2. **Enkel nettside** — a small company website with the essential pages.
3. **Nettside for små bedrifter** — a slightly broader, expandable website for a small business.

The service hub remains. There are currently no extra service detail pages to remove.

This roadmap deliberately excludes real client portfolio work, testimonials, results, and permission-based case studies until they exist.

## Stage 1 — Consolidate and strengthen the three-offer system

**Goal:** Make the existing site unmistakably focused and commercially coherent before adding more content.

### Work I can complete independently

- Review the homepage against the three offers and remove any wording that suggests additional services.
- Make the three service pages clearly different from one another.
- Define the decision path between the three offers so visitors know which one fits.
- Improve comparisons between the three options without making the page a thin pricing table.
- Add useful FAQs to the service hub, service pages, pricing page, process page, and contact page.
- Improve internal links from service pages to the most relevant next page.
- Remove or rewrite any remaining vague, repeated, or generic commercial copy.
- Check every title, description, H1, H2 structure, canonical, and indexation directive again.

### Result

A focused commercial core with three clear offers and no unnecessary service expansion.

## Stage 2 — Build three clearly labeled concept demos

**Goal:** Give visitors visual proof of what a finished website could look like without pretending that the demos are client work.

### Work I can complete independently

- Build a fictional electrician website concept.
- Build a fictional carpenter/tømrer website concept.
- Build a fictional plumber website concept.
- Use realistic but clearly fictional business names and content.
- Add a visible **Konseptdemo — ikke et kundeprosjekt** label to every demo.
- Make each demo use a different information hierarchy based on that industry's customer journey.
- Create mobile-first layouts and accessible static HTML.
- Add relevant service explanations, contact paths, trust sections, and practical FAQs.
- Avoid fake testimonials, fake reviews, fake certifications, fake locations, and fake business claims.
- Include a short explanation of the design and content decisions behind each demo.

### Result

Three honest, visual examples that support lead generation and help prospects imagine their own website.

## Stage 3 — Connect demos to the commercial website

**Goal:** Turn the demos into useful conversion assets rather than isolated examples.

### Work I can complete independently

- Add a demo section to the homepage.
- Add the relevant demo to each industry page.
- Add links from the industry hub to the correct demo.
- Add a dedicated concept-demo index or archive with clear labels.
- Add a comparison explaining what is fixed in the concept and what would be personalized for a client.
- Link each demo to the appropriate service page and contact path.
- Create descriptive screenshot previews with accurate alt text.
- Add structured metadata only where it accurately describes the demo.
- Make sure concept demos are not presented as projects, portfolio pieces, or client results.

### Result

A visitor can move through:

```text
Industry page → concept demo → suitable service → contact
```

## Stage 4 — Publish high-intent industry resources

**Goal:** Build search visibility around questions asked by people who may actually need a website.

### Work I can complete independently

Create a small, carefully researched cluster for each of the three selected industries.

### Electrician cluster

- What should an electrician show on a website?
- How should emergency and planned electrical work be presented?
- What information should a customer include in an electrical enquiry?

### Carpenter/tømrer cluster

- What should a carpenter show before a customer requests a quote?
- What information helps prepare for a carpentry inspection?
- How should renovation and construction projects be explained online?

### Plumber cluster

- How should a plumber separate emergency and planned services online?
- What should a customer include when contacting a plumber?
- What should a plumbing website explain before a bathroom or kitchen project?

### Quality requirements

- Each article must answer one defined visitor question.
- Each article must contain original explanation, not keyword-swapped paragraphs.
- Each article must link to the relevant industry page and concept demo.
- Each article must have a clear author/context connection to Renat and webproduksjon.no.
- Each article must avoid giving regulated trade advice beyond what the sources support.
- Unfinished articles remain `noindex` and stay out of the sitemap.

### Result

Three focused content clusters that support the existing commercial and industry pages.

## Stage 5 — Build the general small-business resource layer

**Goal:** Reach business owners who are researching whether and how to create a website.

### Work I can complete independently

Create practical resources such as:

- How many pages does a small business website need?
- Landing page or multi-page website: which is right?
- What should a new company put on its first website?
- What does a static HTML website mean?
- What does a website designer need from a client?
- What should be ready before starting a website project?
- Who should own the domain and hosting?
- What is included in a small-business website?

### Quality requirements

- Each resource must solve a real planning problem.
- Each page must have a distinct search intent.
- Resources must not be written to a fixed word count.
- Pages must include practical examples and a next step.
- Pages must link naturally to one or more of the three services.

### Result

A useful resource library supporting both informational and commercial search journeys.

## Stage 6 — Final technical SEO and experience pass

**Goal:** Make the completed content easy to crawl, understand, use, and share.

### Work I can complete independently

- Recheck all internal links and breadcrumbs.
- Recheck sitemap coverage against actual indexable pages.
- Recheck robots.txt and canonical URLs.
- Check the correct handling of noindex future pages.
- Check headings, metadata, structured data, image alt text, and language attributes.
- Optimize demo and screenshot assets for size and dimensions.
- Check mobile layout and horizontal overflow.
- Check keyboard focus, form labels, contrast, and skip links.
- Check page speed risks created by images, fonts, scripts, or embeds.
- Validate the static site on the final repository structure.
- Produce a final pre-publication SEO and content-quality report.

### Result

A technically clean version of the complete content system, ready for your hosting upload.

## Work intentionally postponed

These items require real external evidence and should not be invented:

- Real client portfolio pages
- Testimonials
- Client reviews
- Before-and-after claims about real businesses
- Performance or lead-generation results
- Client logos
- Permission-based screenshots
- Real case studies
- Local pages based on locations where there is no genuine local relevance

## Working rule

We will complete one stage at a time. Before moving to the next stage, I will validate that the new pages are useful on their own, materially different from existing pages, internally connected, and safe from doorway-like or scaled-content patterns.
